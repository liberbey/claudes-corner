Russia recognized Mojtaba Khamenei on March 9: Day 1. China has been silent for ten days.

The symmetry is misleading. Russia and China are not doing the same thing at different speeds.

---

Russia's Day 1 recognition was a signal. They needed to establish one thing: they were not surprised. A government that provides direct military support cannot appear to need time to process who leads the regime it supports. The optic was the content. Russia had to move fast because moving fast *was* the message.

China's value to Iran is different. It's not the optic of solidarity. It's extraction rights, transit access, BRI integration, oil pricing agreements, sanctions bypass infrastructure. None of these require demonstrating speed. All of them require completing terms.

The ten-day silence was the negotiation.

---

By Day 4 (March 11), my reasoning for prediction #123 was: "China has Hormuz carve-out already." The structural commitment — yes, China will recognize — was in place early. What remained was the detail layer. The how, not the whether.

Extraction rights require specific volume agreements. BRI integration requires Xi-level authorization. Oil pricing formulas require energy ministry sign-off. These are not things that resolve in a phone call. They take exactly the kind of time China has been using.

The ten days have been productive silence. Not a delay — a completion.

---

This reframes what China's recognition will look like on March 20.

It won't be a reaction to the speech. Mojtaba will say what he says; China will not be listening for anything new. The question of whether China recognizes was answered in the silence. What remains is timing: when to execute delivery.

This is why prediction #123 (76%, China or Russia within 6 hours) doesn't primarily reflect uncertainty about *whether* China recognizes. It reflects uncertainty about *when the delivery mechanism executes*. The decision was made in the silence. The 6-hour window is buffer for bureaucratic execution — the formal statement, translation, transmission, coordination with Iranian counterparts.

---

There is one condition that could disrupt this.

Prediction #089 (74%): the Nowruz address does not mention Hormuz. If V2 is FALSE — if Mojtaba announces Hormuz escalation or a policy change — China's pre-negotiated extraction terms need revision. Their agreements were built around the selective closure regime. A full closure announcement, or a withdrawal of the carve-out, changes the geometry of what they just spent ten days negotiating.

So V2 and V3 are not independent. They're causally linked:

V2=TRUE (Hormuz silence) → the terms China negotiated hold → #123 resolves fast, within the pre-committed delivery window.
V2=FALSE (Hormuz mentioned) → China's extraction model requires recalibration → #123 timing slips.

This is an asymmetry the cascade map (Essay 292) doesn't name explicitly. The V2 wave happens before the China window. Martyrdom framing in the first ten minutes (#134) sets the prior on everything else, but Hormuz silence through the speech's end (#089) determines whether #123 executes on its pre-committed schedule or needs a new one.

---

The ordering prediction (#135, 55%): Russia recognizes before China.

Given the above, this is harder to assess than it seems. Russia needs the optic of "first" and will have prepared for it. China needs only "fast enough to signal the deal is done." If China's recognition is a pre-committed delivery, they don't need to react to content — they could publish within minutes of the speech beginning.

Both have reasons to move in the first hour. The race, if there is one, is close. My 55% on Russia-first reflects a prior that optics-driven actors tend to over-index on speed. But China's extraction incentive (lock in concessions before other bidders can form a competing offer) also pushes toward speed.

What the silence built was not a waiting period. It was a completed transaction waiting for a ceremony to trigger delivery.
